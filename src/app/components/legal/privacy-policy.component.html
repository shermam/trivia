Trivimind is a trivia game. This policy describes what information the app handles, why it handles it, and who else sees it. Everything below about data was written by reading the application's own source code, so it describes what the app actually does rather than what a generic policy template assumes.

The short version: you can play without an account, the app has no analytics or advertising of any kind, it sets no cookies, and nothing about you is sold or shared. The leaderboard and the community question bank are public — everything else is not.

Information you give us

Playing without an account

The moment you open Trivimind, before you interact with anything, the app creates an anonymous account for you. It is a random identifier with no personal information attached to it. It exists so you can play and so your session survives closing the tab. You are never asked to sign in to play.

Signing in

Signing in is optional. It is only needed to save a score to the public leaderboard, or to subscribe to Pro. If you sign in from an anonymous session, that same account is upgraded rather than a second one being created, so anything already saved carries over.

  • Email and password. We store your email address, and whether it has been verified — saving a score requires a verified address. Your password is handled entirely by Firebase Authentication and never passes through our own code. If you ask for a password reset, the reset email is sent by Firebase; we are told nothing about whether you opened it.
  • Google, Facebook, GitHub, Microsoft, Apple, Twitter/X or Yahoo. The provider gives us your email address, your display name, and an account identifier. We request nothing beyond that and never receive your password. Your browser only contacts the provider at the moment you click one of these buttons — not on page load.
  • Display name. Optional and set by you. If you save a score, this name is shown publicly on the leaderboard, so choose it accordingly.

Questions you contribute

Pro subscribers can add questions to the shared question bank. Once a question is approved, its text, the answers, the category, the difficulty and — if you provide them — the source link, the source name and the justification are published publicly and shown to every other player. Your account identifier is stored alongside each question as its author. Treat anything you submit as permanently public, and please don't put personal information about yourself or anyone else into a question — see the Terms for what happens to contributions if you later delete your account.

Submissions are reviewed before they are shown to other players. A question you submit is stored straight away and is readable by our reviewers, but it is not served in anyone's game until a reviewer approves it. A question that is not approved stays stored and simply is not served; it is not deleted, and your account identifier stays attached to it either way.

A question may carry a link to a source, supplied by whoever contributed it. Following one takes you to a website we do not run and are not responsible for, and what happens there is governed by that site's own privacy policy, not this one. We do not tell it where you came from: these links are marked so that your browser sends no referrer.

There is currently no way to edit or withdraw a question from within the app; to have one removed, write to the address above.

Reports you file about a question

Any signed-in player can flag a question and file a report. The report stores the question's identifier, the reason you chose, anything you typed in the optional detail box, and your account identifier. Reports are readable by us and by the small number of players we have appointed as reviewers, who read them in the app in order to act on what they say — that is how a report reaches somebody who can do something about the question. No other player can read them. The review screen does not show who filed a report, though your account identifier stays part of the record a reviewer's access reaches.

Information created by using the app

Leaderboard entries — these are public

When you save a score we store your display name, your score for that game, how many questions it had, the share you answered correctly, and when you saved it. The leaderboards are readable by anyone, including people who are not signed in. There is a separate board for each time limit — 15 seconds, 30 seconds and no limit — so you may hold up to one entry on each, and each keeps only your best score.

The save form also offers a country to rank in, and it is optional. Choose one and your score is published a second time, on that country's board: the country you chose is stored on that entry and shown beside your name and score, readable by anyone in the same way. It is whatever you selected — we never publish a country we worked out for you, only one you picked, and the dropdown starts on the country described below purely so it is usually already right. Leave it on “Prefer not to say” and only the global board gets your score. Your choice is remembered on your device so you do not have to make it every game; changing it affects future saves only, and an entry already published under a country stays where it is until you delete your account.

Your gameplay totals — these are private to you

Once you have finished a game while signed in, we keep a single record of your running totals: how many games you have finished, how many questions you have been asked, how many you got right, your longest run of correct answers within one game, when those totals started being kept, and when they were last updated. It also holds the identifier of the most recent game and a short-lived count of how many games you have banked in the past hour, both of which exist to stop the same game being counted twice or the totals being inflated.

Only you can read it. It is never shown to other players and never appears on a leaderboard. We keep no per-game history: not what you were asked, not what you answered, and no record of any individual game beyond the most recent one's identifier and the time it was banked. Nothing is kept at all until you finish your first game while signed in, and nothing is kept for anonymous play. Deleting your account deletes it, and “Export my data” returns it in full.

Subscription records

If you subscribe to Pro we store the status of your subscription, which plan and price it is for, and whether it is set to cancel at the end of the period. This includes no card details — see below.

Which country you are in — to fill in two things you can change

On two screens the app asks its own server which country your IP address maps to. On the pricing page it decides which currency the price is shown in, because a card issued in Brazil is declined outright if it is charged in dollars. On the game-over screen it decides which country the leaderboard dropdown opens on — and only which one it opens on: what is published is whatever the dropdown says when you press Save, so nothing worked out about you is ever stored or shown. It happens nowhere else, and neither screen asks your device for its location; the browser's location permission is switched off for this site altogether.

Firebase Hosting works the country out at its edge and passes it to us as a two-letter country code; that code is the whole of what we receive and the whole of what is sent back to your browser — not your IP address, not a city, and nothing read from your device's own location services, which the app never asks for.

We keep none of it. The code is read off the request, sent to your browser and forgotten: not written to the database, not written to a log, not attached to your account, and not put in a cookie — there are none. Your browser keeps it for a day, in its own local storage, so a page can open on the right answer straight away next time; it stays on your device, it is never sent back to us, and clearing site data removes it. All it ever does is decide what a control starts on — the currency switch beside the price, or the country dropdown on the save form — and you can change either. In particular a country we worked out is never published on a leaderboard and never written to your account; only a country you selected yourself is. No third-party geolocation service is involved, because the request goes to Trivimind's own address: nobody outside the services listed further down learns that you looked at the page.

Payments

Payments are processed entirely by Stripe. We never see, receive or store your card number. The first time you open the pricing page signed in with a verified account and not yet subscribed, we send Stripe your email address and an internal account identifier and ask it to prepare a checkout page for you, so that pressing Subscribe does not leave you waiting. That happens whether or not you go on to subscribe; an unused checkout page expires within 24 hours, and nothing is charged unless you complete it. The identifier is what lets a subscription be matched to your Trivimind account. Everything after that — the payment form, the billing portal, your saved payment methods — happens on Stripe's own systems under Stripe's privacy policy.

What is stored on your device

Trivimind sets no cookies. It uses your browser's own storage instead:

  • Local storage — your light/dark theme preference; whether you have muted the game's sound effects, which is a single yes-or-no that stays on this device and is sent nowhere; your sign-in session (managed by Firebase Authentication); and, once you have opened the pricing page, what Pro costs in each currency it is sold in, together with the two-letter country code described above. Those last two are kept so the page can show you a price straight away on your next visit instead of asking again, and both are discarded after 24 hours.
  • Local storage, once you have chosen a country to rank in — that country's two-letter code, so the save form does not ask you again every game. Only a country you chose yourself is stored here: if you have never touched the dropdown, or set it back to “Prefer not to say”, nothing is kept and the guess described above is made afresh next time. It stays on this device and is never sent anywhere on its own; what reaches our database is the leaderboard entry you save under it.
  • Local storage, if you are signed in with a verified account and not yet subscribed — the web address of the Stripe Checkout page prepared for you while you read the pricing page, so that pressing Subscribe takes you straight there instead of making you wait. It is a link and nothing else, and it is never sent anywhere. It is kept on this device for up to 20 hours, and is discarded when you sign out or when a newer one replaces it. It is stored where every tab can see it deliberately: Stripe allows one checkout page open at a time, so a second tab that could not see this one would replace it and break the first.
  • IndexedDB — up to 200 trivia questions you have already been served, so a game can start with no network connection; and a snapshot of a game in progress, so a reload or a crash doesn't lose it. The saved game is discarded after six hours. Neither contains personal information beyond the game itself.
  • IndexedDB, once you have answered a question — a list of the questions you have already answered, so that a later game can ask you something else. Each entry is an identifier and the moment you answered: for a question from the community bank the identifier is that question's own reference, and for one from Open Trivia DB it is a short fingerprint of its wording. Nothing records which answer you gave or whether you were right. It holds at most 2,000 entries and the oldest go first. It stays on this device: it is never sent anywhere, it is not tied to your account, and playing on another device starts again.
  • Service worker cache — the app's own files (scripts, styles, icons), so it loads offline and can be installed to your home screen.
  • A count of the games you have played today, so the free tier's daily limit can be applied. It is a number and a date, nothing more — not a history of what you played, and not tied to your account. It stays on this device: no count of your games is sent anywhere, and playing on another device starts again.

All of it is removed if you clear site data for this site in your browser.

What we do not do

Trivimind has no analytics, no advertising, no tracking pixels and no third-party marketing tools of any kind. We do not sell your information, we do not share it for advertising, and we do not build a profile of you. The services in the table above are there because the app cannot function without them, not to observe you.

Where your information is stored

The database is hosted in Google Cloud's nam5 multi-region, in the United States. Firebase Authentication and the server functions are operated by Google and may process data in the United States as well. Stripe processes payment data on its own infrastructure, which is also primarily in the United States.

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Brazil, this means your information is transferred outside your country. Those transfers rely on the data-processing terms and standard contractual clauses we have in place with Google and Stripe as our processors. You can ask us for details of these at the contact address above.

How long we keep it

We would rather describe this accurately than quote a period we don't actually enforce. What is true today:

  • Your account, leaderboard entries, gameplay totals and billing records are kept for as long as your account exists. Delete your account and they go — see below.
  • Checkout and billing-portal records expire 24 hours after they are created and are then deleted automatically. The sweep is best-effort, so treat 24 hours as the earliest they go, not a guarantee of the latest.
  • A game saved in your browser is discarded after six hours; the offline question pool is capped at 200 questions, oldest first.
  • Questions you contributed stay in the shared bank indefinitely, including after you delete your account, with your authorship removed. This is the one place where deletion is not complete, and it is deliberate — the reasoning is in the next section and in the Terms.
  • Reports you file about a question are kept indefinitely, including your account identifier, and are not removed when you delete your account. A report is a record that something needed looking at; if it vanished when its author left, the abuse signal would go with it.
  • The country your IP address maps to is not kept for any length of time, because it is not kept at all — it is read from the request that asks for it and discarded with the answer. A country you chose in the save form is different: it is part of the leaderboard entry you published under it, and it is kept for as long as that entry is, which is until you delete your account.
  • Anonymous accounts that are never signed in to hold no personal information, and no data of any kind is attached to them. Firebase deletes them automatically after 30 days without activity.

Your rights over your information

Two of the important ones are built into the app, in the account menu, and need no request to us:

  • Download my data gives you a JSON file containing your account details, your leaderboard entries on every board — global and country alike — your gameplay totals, every question you have contributed, and your billing records. It also lists what is deliberately not in it and why.
  • Delete account cancels any live subscription, deletes your Stripe customer record and the payment methods stored against it, deletes your leaderboard entries on every board, country boards included, deletes your gameplay totals, deletes your billing records, and deletes your sign-in account itself. Questions you contributed are kept, with your authorship replaced by a [deleted-user] marker. They are part of a bank other players are drawing on; what goes is the link between them and you. This is irreversible, and there is no recovery period.

Depending on where you live you may also have the right to ask us to correct information, to restrict or object to how we use it, to receive it in a portable form, or to complain to a data protection authority — in Brazil, the ANPD; in the EEA or UK, your national authority. To exercise any of these, write to quizloop.trivia@gmail.com. We aim to reply within 15 days, which is the shortest deadline that applies to us.

You do not have to give us anything beyond an account to play. An email address is only required if you want to save a score or subscribe.

Children

Trivimind is a general-audience game and is not directed at children. You must be 13 or older to use it, as set out in the Terms. We do not knowingly collect information from anyone under 13, and there is no age verification — the app asks for a date of birth nowhere.

If you are a parent or guardian and believe a child under 13 has created an account, write to quizloop.trivia@gmail.com and we will delete it and its data.

How your information is protected

Rather than describe this in adjectives, here is what is actually in place. Everything is served over HTTPS only. Access to every record in the database is enforced by server-side rules, not by the app's interface, so a request that bypasses the app entirely is refused the same way — your billing records, for instance, are readable only by you, and a report about a question is readable only by us and by the reviewers appointed to act on it. Passwords are handled by Firebase Authentication and never reach our own code. Payment card details never reach us at all. The app sends a strict Content Security Policy that names every host the browser is allowed to contact and forbids being embedded in a frame by anyone.

No system is perfectly secure, and we don't claim otherwise. If you find a security problem, please tell us at quizloop.trivia@gmail.com before disclosing it publicly.
